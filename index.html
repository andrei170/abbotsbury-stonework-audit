Recruitment Audit · Abbotsbury Stonework Ltd · South Devon

Booked Out Until Next Year. The Only Thing Stopping More Work Is Masons.

Prepared for Lyndon Punter · Abbotsbury Stonework Ltd · 16 September 2026 · AG1 Consulting
0 / 10
pages on your website that speak to a mason looking for work

You do not have a demand problem. You have a diary full into next year and no way of adding hands to it.

Every extra qualified mason is more jobs out of the diary and into the ground. But good stonemasons are rare, most are already employed, and almost none of them are sitting on a job board.

So the question is not "where do we advertise". It is "how do we reach masons who are not looking, and give them a reason to move".

Finding 1 — The Whole Trade Is Short Of Hands
Historic England & CITB — Skills Needs Analysis 2024 (traditional pre-1919 buildings)
"More than 40% reported that it had become more difficult to recruit over the last three years with only 4% saying it was easier." "Nearly a fifth had reported turning down work due to skills shortages."

You are in that fifth. Booked until next year means work waiting that could be earning now if the crew were bigger.

The upside of a shortage: the firm that makes itself the obvious place to work gets first pick of the few masons who are available — and of the ones who would move for the right job.

Finding 2 — The Masons You Want Are Not On Job Boards
Stonemason vacancies, as listed on the day we checked (15 Sept 2026): Totaljobs — "stonemason, South West" 2 results CV-Library — "stonemason, Devon" 1 result What that tells you: almost nobody recruits masons through job boards so almost no masons go looking on them
The good ones are already working for someone else

An advert on a board that stonemasons do not check will not find you a stonemason. The people you want are on a site in Totnes or Exeter today, scrolling their phone at lunch. Reaching them means putting the job in front of people who already do the work, in South Devon, whether or not they are job hunting.

Finding 3 — Your Website Sells To Customers, Not To Masons
Pages on abbotsburystonework.co.uk: 10 Careers / jobs / vacancies page: none (/careers/, /jobs/, /vacancies/ all 404) What your site already says — that a mason would care about: "City and Guilds qualified Stonemasons" "team ... holds CSCS Advanced Craft Certificates" "Our highly skilled team have been with us since the beginning"
You already have the best recruiting line in the trade — and it is aimed at customers

"Our team have been with us since the beginning." For a mason deciding whether to move, that is the single most reassuring thing an employer can say: people stay here. Right now it sits on a page written for homeowners.

A mason who hears about the job and looks you up finds nothing about the work, the stone, the areas, the pay or what it is like to work for you. If they cannot picture the job, they do not move.

Finding 4 — "As Many As Possible" Needs A Shape
The roles, and the questions that sort them before anyone takes your time: Qualified mason City & Guilds / NVQ level ....... which level Drystone waller CSCS card ........................ which colour Improver / labourer Years in the trade ............... how many Apprentice Stone worked (granite, limestone, slate, Devon red sandstone) ...... which Driving licence / own transport .. yes / no Can reach South Devon sites ...... yes / no
If you only have time for one conversation, it should be with the right person

"As many as possible" is the right ambition. But a finished mason, a waller, an improver and an apprentice are four different adverts, four different messages and four different rates. Written down, the questions do the sorting while you are on site — and every call you take is with someone who could actually start.

What this tells you before you write an advert

The agencies advertising for masons in Devon are offering £18 to £25 an hour — well above the national employee median of £16.41, because experienced masons are scarce. A mason who already has steady work will compare you with those numbers.

A labourer in South Devon is being offered around £16–£18 an hour. If a finished mason is not clearly ahead of that, the move is not worth it to them.

And almost nobody is advertising — we found no stonemasonry apprenticeship and no drystone waller vacancy anywhere in the South West. An apprentice route would have the field to itself.

Small Fixes While You Are Here
Unrelated to hiring, each a few minutes
  • The "Stone Paving" card on your homepage links to http://stone-paving — an address that does not exist. Anyone who taps it gets an error instead of your paving page.
  • The privacy policy page returns "not found" (/privacy-policy/ is a 404), and the footer text is not linked.
  • Your experience is described three ways on the homepage alone — "over 24 years", "over 20 years" and "over 17 years", all on the same page, with "Founded in 2000" on the About page. Pick one and use it everywhere.
  • No reviews anywhere. No testimonials on the site, and your Facebook page shows no ratings. For a firm with over twenty years of walls standing, that is the easiest proof you are not using.
  • No Google Analytics. The site only has a basic visitor counter, so a recruitment page could not be measured properly.

The Method, In Four Steps
  • Go where masons already are. Put the job in front of people who already work in stone across South Devon — not the handful browsing job boards.
  • Give them a reason to move. A careers page with the work, the stone, the areas, the pay and the fact that your team stays.
  • Let the questions sort them. Level, card, years, stone, transport — before anyone takes your time.
  • Build the pipeline behind them. An apprentice route, so next year you are not starting from zero again.

The work is already booked. The masons are the missing piece.

Every qualified hand you add is jobs out of next year's diary and into this one.
